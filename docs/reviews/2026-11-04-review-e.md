**Is the score race designed out?** On the server, yes, and I could not find a path left. The design has the three parts the item names, and I checked each:
- One fact. `game.ts` keeps one record per player and question; the tablet's `mine` in its view, the acknowledgement's `record`, the reveal's winners and distribution, the scores and the final ranking are all computed from it (`recordOf`, `scores`, `winners`, `distribution`). After a player's last acknowledged tap nothing but that player's own tap can change it.
- It settles. `answerOutcome` refuses a tap once the class has left the question's reveal (the ranking, the next question, the final ranking, a finish, a skip), except the resend of what is already stored. So a podium can no longer change after it was shown; the old open end ("counted however late, after the final ranking too") is closed. Probe and unit tests agree.
- The tablet's marks. A tap is a dashed, pulsing outline until the tablet's own view carries it as stored, then solid; a refused tap falls back to the view. `markChosen` edits the classes in place so a redraw doesn't swallow a tap. In `play.ts` I traced the cases that could leave it dashed (view before ack, two taps in a row, a refused second tap, a tap whose question has gone): none does.

Two limits on how far "by construction" reaches, neither a bug:
- The browser tablet never reads the acknowledgement's `record` (`grep` finds it only in `simulate.mjs`); it shows what its next view says. That is the same store, so the fact is one, but the simulator's tablets learn by a path the real tablet doesn't use. And nothing in the simulator compares a tablet's view (`mine`, `reveal.mine`) with what the server scores; the final view has none. The dashed-then-solid behaviour was seen only on one localhost tablet in the walk, not under dropped lines. A check at the quiet point, "each device's last `mine` equals its acknowledged record equals what scored", would close the claim at small cost.
- One narrow disagreement remains in how the server remembers, older than this branch: a repeat of the same answer moves `seq` in memory only (`prev.seq = seq`, not persisted), so after an instance restart a delayed different tap with a seq between the two could win over the child's last tap. The score and the tablet's next view would still agree, only not with the child's intent. Not worth acting on.

**Findings, most important first**

The merged backlog contradicts itself (verified by `git show` of the merge tree, 2bc09ab).** Main's item 6 keeps "A first build on branch `tempo-bar` was given these decisions piecemeal while in flight; a fresh implementer restarts from this item", and the branch's added paragraph "As built on branch `tempo-bar` … with the owner's revision of the bar" follows the History paragraph. Delete the "restarts" sentence, and fold or drop the "As built" paragraph to one line pointing at the decisions entry. The branch's other backlog edits (the score race "designed out", the real-device list) are fine.

**2. An accidental Last call can't be taken back (design, not run on a real hand).**
- Last call is the left button of the pair, 34 % of the Remote's row, next to Play; on the wall 8.4 em beside Play's 10.2. The first press from Question shows the answers *and* starts the clock, so a thumb that meant Show answers gives the class fifteen seconds. Once running the button is greyed. The only ways out are Lock in (which ends answering at once) or waiting; there is no longer a "Stop the countdown" (it went with the tempo sheet, the decisions entry's reasoning being that the clock is the teacher's own press).
- It isn't a failure of the item, which doesn't say. But the old bar had the valve and the new one hasn't; a second press of Last call cancelling it (back to open answers, the greyed state becoming a toggle) would be the smallest answer. Reopening after Lock in stays out, as before (it copies the majority).
- Related and by design: a second, lone press 450 to about 500 ms after Show answers is not blocked (`Presses`, fake clock: Show answers at t, Play at t+600 sends Lock in with nobody answered). The guard stops the doubled and the instant press, as the item asks; it can't know a nervous second press from a wanted one. Whether 400/450 ms suit a hand and a clicker is the entry's own "not established", and I agree it needs a real one.

**3. A blocked press says nothing (verified, by accident).** A press inside the settle window, or a Play right after Last call, returns silently. My own script clicked Start 0.2 s after the join screen first appeared and nothing happened; I only found out because the wall stayed on the join screen. For the item's purpose ("a bounce or a click on a screen that just moved does nothing") that is right; for a teacher who presses once and sees no change, there is no hint. A 400 ms window is short enough that people will rarely hit it, but on a slow line the window starts when the view *arrives* (as designed), so a teacher who watches nothing move for a second and presses again is not blocked, and one who presses right after it moves is. I'd leave it, and check it with a real hand.

**4. Last call doesn't lock in at once when everyone has already answered (verified).** `closeIfAllIn` runs when a tap is recorded and on a teacher tab's tick (the Remote ticks every 20 s while its tab is visible, a steering wall every 15 s). In the probe the only player answered, then Last call was pressed: still open with the clock running 3.5 s later. Pressing Last call after the whole class answered is rare (Lock in is the button then), and the case that matters, the last child answering during Last call, does lock in. Worth knowing for "Last call, then the last two children's tablets go away": the tick closes it, within 20 s, or the 15 s, whichever first.

**6. `Time:` and `Tempo:` are now dropped without a word, also from question text (verified in `format.ts:364`).** The branch keeps the keyword match and drops the line "like a note", to let a quiz from a cached guidance in. Before, a `Time:` line that wasn't a length raised "was ignored" and a clamped one a warning. A quiz about telling the time or about tempo in music whose question text starts a line with "Time:" or "Tempo:" loses that line and the teacher isn't told. Rare, since a question line normally starts with its number; if it matters, drop silently only when the value reads as a tempo or a length, and keep the old warning otherwise.

**7. The guidance describes the bar correctly but not what it asks of the AI.** The lesson paragraph and the guidance's limits match the bar (Play labels, Last call, the double press, "pressed twice in quick succession, Play takes two steps, but never past the start of a question"). What it doesn't say to the AI writing questions is that every question is now read out *without its answers* before they show, which makes a stem like "Which of these …?" or a question that points at "the figure below the answers" read badly aloud. One sentence in "What informs the shape" would do. Also: "A presentation clicker and the space bar press Play" is true when the wall steers itself; with a Remote the wall takes only a clicker's keys (PageDown, →, ↓) and Space does nothing, as before.

At 320 the ranking state is still wider than the screen: the page widens by about 10 px and Play moves (x 176–314 in Show answers to 186–324 in Next), the same kind of overflow the `min(6em, 17vw)` fix removed at 360. The brief names 360; I'd treat 320 as a rare phone and leave it.

**Load, and the stranger**
- Cost. The entry says it: 1520 requests for a class's run in `--all-poll` against 822 on the votes branch, "not like for like" (five states a question instead of three, each a view to every device). Mine: 1479. The landing and `/own-copy` say "room for a few dozen classes a day", derived in `limits.ts` from about 30 classes a day when every device polls. Nobody has recomputed that figure for the new flow; for a class that is all long-polling it is nearer 17. Most classes are mostly WebSockets (the simulator has 5 of 25 polling), so the effect on the real allowance is smaller; the sentence is the owner's to confirm. Storage writes rise by two a question out of about thirty, which is small.
- What a stranger can do with this branch: nothing new that I found. The new commands (`play`, `lastCall`, `speed`) sit behind the teacher-role check; a screen that doesn't steer is told "The remote steers the quiz." for `lastCall` and `speed`, and its `play` is the clicker's, as before. The acknowledgement's new `record` is the sender's own answer, for any `q` (null outside the quiz). An old cached page gets "unknown command" for the commands that are gone and keeps `go`, `votes`, `reveal`, `next`, `ranking` and `advance`, which still work (`advance` from the lobby still begins the quiz, only for a screen role, as before).
- One thing the switch makes matter: points for speed trust the device's own time. The server caps a claim at what it counted but can't tell a claim of 0 ms from a true one, so a child with the developer tools open gets the full bonus on every right answer. Unchanged by this branch and the owner's earlier decision ("device time as before"); now it counts in every class that turns the switch on.
