# classyQuiz — Avatars the students find interesting

Backlog v2 item 5. **Revision 1, 2026-11-03: the design the owner reacted
to ("good changes").** His answers to §8 went with the recommendations:
dress the assigned animal, shapes and hats without tools, a plain start,
fixed at Start. His drawing notes: the hats fitted the frog poorly, the
wand's star sat off centre, and the penguin's hats could be smaller.
**Built** as below; what was built and the evidence are in decisions.md,
"Avatars the children dress". The figures in `docs/avatars/` show the
design as he saw it, before the frog and penguin changes.
**Since:** the lion's mane is drawn smaller, so a shape reads behind it as
behind the other animals (backlog v4 item 9; the measurement is in
decisions.md, "The winners jump; the lion's mane; the lobby's wiggle";
figure `lion-before-after.png`).

**The proposal.** An avatar does two jobs: it tells the children apart, and
it belongs to a child. Today random assignment does both. Keep the first
job assigned: the animal and its colour stay as they are, unique and drawn
by the server. Give the second job to the child: while the class is
still joining, a child can **dress their animal**, with a background shape
and a hat, from one menu that is the same for everybody. It's optional,
it has no words, and the child is on the wall before touching it. It shows
on the wall live, and it is fixed at Start.

## 1. What decides it

**Where fights come from.** Children fight over something scarce that
another child holds or could get, and over goods that aren't equal.
- Random assignment has neither. Nothing can be grabbed, and nobody chose
  the frog, so there is nobody to blame for it.
- Picking the identity makes it scarce: two children can't both be the
  purple frog, so offers or picks collide.
- Rare or earned items make goods unequal.
- Decorations from a shared menu are **non-rival**: any number of children
  can wear the crown, no item is better than another, and nothing is
  earned. A child has nothing to take away from another.

**Where the time goes.** Reaching the site and typing the code is the
slowest step (v1 item 4). Tablets arrive over about a minute, so the early
ones wait. Today that wait is a frog and an eye icon. Dressing fills it and
is never on the path, because no tap stands between the code and being on
the wall. The last child to arrive has no time to dress and plays plain,
like everyone today.

**What makes it interesting.** Three things, from background knowledge
and not checked this session; the studies are mostly on adults.
- **Ownership from choice.** Something a person chose or put together is
  valued more than the same thing handed out (choice-induced preference,
  the "IKEA effect").
- **The big screen as a mirror.** What a child taps shows up on the wall
  a second later, with a wiggle. That moment is the payoff: "look, I'm the
  star frog!" It also teaches the child where they are on the wall, since
  a child finds themselves by what moves when they act.
- **A livelier lobby wall**, and a ranking with more character.

**The function it serves too.** At the who-answered bar's sizes (21–34 px,
decisions.md "Spread, then key") a child mostly sees the colour and the
outline; the animal barely reads (figure `wall-bar.png`).
- A background shape changes the outline, which is the cue that survives
  at 26 px. Hats add a coloured blob, and tools vanish.
- A child who knows "purple star" searches among the purple ones that are
  stars, not among every purple avatar.
- If half the class picks the star, it narrows less, but it is never
  worse than today.

## 2. The options against the owner's three criteria

| | Time | Fights | Interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Random (today)** | none | none | the animal you got |
| **Pick from three** | a tap for every child before they appear; a dithering child holds the class, and to the teacher looks not yet joined | the offers must be reserved, or one child loses a race; neighbours compare offers ("you got the lion"), with the scarce one visible on someone else's tablet | strongest on ownership: it's the animal itself |
| **Customise the identity** (animal, colour) | several steps before appearing | "purple frog is taken" | high |
| **Random, then swap in the lobby** | off the path | two children want the one free lion; the wall reshuffles | medium |
| **Earned items** (a hat per right answer) | none | status | shows performance on the who-answered bar: against "exposes nobody" and the top-three ranking |
| **Rare random items** | none | envy of the golden one, which can't be changed | surprise |
| **Kept on the device across lessons** | none | none | continuity; against shared tablets and "nothing kept" |
| **Generated names** ("Captain Frog") | none | none | high for readers; young readers and the quiz's language make it a localisation item |
| **Dress the assigned animal (proposed)** | none on the path; uses the lobby wait | non-rival; the identity stays assigned | ownership of the look, the wall as a mirror |

Pick-from-three has the one advantage the proposal lacks: ownership of the
animal itself. Its costs land on the two criteria random scores highest
on. Section 8 asks the owner whether he wants it anyway.

## 3. What a child sees and does

Figure `tablet-lobby.png`: a 960×600 tablet, a 600×960 tablet, and a 360 px
phone.

1. **The address, the code, ▶.** Unchanged. This is the slowest step, about
   20–60 s.
2. **Their animal, large, on today's disc.** It is already on the wall. Below
   it are two rows of pictures:
   - six background shapes, in the child's own colour;
   - eight hats.

   The eye icon stays. There are no words.
3. **Tapping a shape** puts the animal on it, on the tablet at once and on
   the wall about a second later, where it wiggles.
   - **Tapping a hat** puts it on; tapping the hat it wears takes it off.
   - The child can change as often as they like until Start.
4. **Start:** the rows go, and the question's buttons come as today. The
   look is fixed for the run.

- **Time added to joining: none.** The time spent dressing is the lobby
  wait.
- **The teacher's line, if they want one:** "While we wait you can dress
  your animal. Look at the board." A child who ignores it plays plain.
- **The teacher's controls:** nothing new. The teacher still decides when
  to Start, and Start ends the dressing. A teacher who gives the class
  another 30 seconds to dress spends lesson time on purpose; nothing on the
  page asks for it.
- **Late joiners** (the door after Start, decisions.md "Review C") get no
  rows and play plain. Dressing is lobby time, and the question is up.
- **Plain start, not a random outfit.** Nobody is shown in something they
  didn't pick. Picture a heart and a flower drawn by chance for a boy who
  arrived last, in a primary class. A latecomer then looks like today's
  avatars.

## 4. What can go wrong between two children

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| Both want the crown | Both wear it; the animals and colours still differ |
| "You copied me" | Nothing can be taken away; the teacher in the room |
| A neighbour dresses someone else's animal | Its owner changes it back until Start. No item is a put-down: no dunce cap, no clown nose. The teacher walks over, as the backlog says |
| Teasing over a choice (heart, flower) | It was the child's own choice, and the plain start shows nobody in a pick that isn't theirs. The teacher |
| Envy of the animal | As today. Dressing is the consolation; there is no swap |
| A child dresses instead of listening | Start ends it |
| A child hammers the hats | The tablet sends the latest look, not every tap. The server caps the stored changes (§7). The wall wiggles per stored change |
| A tablet dies | "Which one are you?" shows the dressed animal, which makes it easier to find. The look is kept with the player |
| Two children, one tablet | One avatar, dressed by agreement or not; the teacher |

Nothing new identifies anyone. The look is three small numbers on the
player and goes with the run.

## 5. The wall, the ranking, the teacher's screens

- **Wall lobby** (figure `wall-lobby.png`; the layout is an approximation,
  the avatars are real): the avatars are dressed, and a changed one is
  redrawn with a short wiggle. Today the lobby only adds newcomers.
- **Who has answered:** dressed avatars, popping in as today. The box sizes
  don't change. A star or a heart covers less of the box than the disc
  does, but the animal stays the same size, so the bar's fit and "nothing
  moves" hold.
- **Ranking** (top three) and the tablet's own place: dressed.
- **The Remote's Players and "Waiting for", and "Which one are you?":**
  dressed.
- **No new controls** for the teacher. The menu has nothing to moderate.

## 6. The set

Figures `shapes-and-tools.png` and `hats.png`.

**Background shapes (6)** ("backdrops" in the figures):
- **In the set:** today's disc, which is the default, plus hexagon, star,
  heart, flower and pond.
- **Out:** rhombus and square from the owner's list, and the triangle. The
  answer marks are a red triangle, a blue diamond, a yellow circle and a
  green square, and the wall's bar holds avatars above the answer tiles
  while the class answers. A blue avatar on a rhombus in the bar reads as
  "answered blue diamond", and the bar was built never to show an
  answer's colour.
- **The disc** is already a circle in eight colours, yellow included. The
  animal covers most of it, and nobody has read it as an answer so far.
- **A cloud was tried and dropped:** at 26 px it reads as a disc.

**Hats (8):** crown, top hat, cap, wizard's hat, party hat, flower,
beanie, viking helmet.
- None is a put-down, and none outranks another. The crown is the obvious
  favourite, and everyone can have it.
- One hat at a time.

**Tools held to the side: recommended against for now** (right half of
`shapes-and-tools.png`, bottom row of `wall-bar.png`).
- The animals are heads without paws, so a tool floats beside the disc
  rather than being held.
- At 21–34 px a staff, pitchfork or shovel is 1–2 px wide, which a
  projector won't show.
- A tool widens the outline on one side only.
- A third row also makes the picker half as long again.
- The balloon and the lantern do survive as blobs. If the owner wants
  tools, those two plus chunkier redraws are the start.

## 7. Cost

**Requests** (Free plan: 100,000 a day for Functions and for Durable
Objects):
- **On WebSockets,** a stored change is one incoming message, billed at
  20:1 (decisions.md, "Runner: hosting shape"). It is followed by a new
  view to the wall, the Remote and the child's tablet. I take outgoing
  messages as unbilled from Cloudflare's pricing as I know it, not checked
  this session.
- **A typical class:** say five changes per child, 125 per class, which
  comes to about 6 request-equivalents.
- **On the HTTP fallback,** each change is a POST (one Function request and
  one Durable Object request), plus a wake for a polling wall or Remote.
  That is up to about 375 of each per class, against about 3,000 for a
  lesson today: under +15%.
- **The tablet sends at once when nothing is in flight.** Otherwise it
  holds the latest look and sends that next. A burst of taps becomes a few
  sends, and the first change still reaches the wall quickly.
- **Cap: 30 stored changes per child,** like the 10 answer changes per
  question. Past it the rows stay and stop responding; only a hammering
  child gets there.

**Storage** (100,000 rows a day; a class of 25 costs about 1,300 today):
- Each stored step writes the run and its meta, which is 2 rows.
- **Unbatched,** a typical class adds about 250 rows (+20%). The worst case
  under the cap is 25 × 30 × 2 = 1,500, more than a whole class.
- **Proposed: the server writes the class's looks at most once a second.**
  The views go out after the write, as now: nothing shows that isn't
  stored. The worst case becomes about 600 rows for a 5-minute lobby of
  hammering, and typically under 250.
- The cost is a second's delay on the wall, which the mirror effect
  tolerates.

**Data:** three small numbers per player, `b` and `h` beside `a` and `c` in
`Avatar`. They are deleted with the run. Pages from before the deploy
ignore the new fields and draw plain.

**Drawing work** (most of it done in the prototype):
- The shapes are independent of the animal.
- Each hat is drawn once and seated per animal: 12 seat positions, and
  `hats.png` is the 96-combination review sheet. The frog, owl, rabbit
  and mouse need a second look: small heads, ears and eye bulges where a
  hat goes.
- The picker's icons come from the same drawings.

**Code:**
- `protocol.ts`: the fields.
- `game.ts`: a lobby-only `dress` command with the cap.
- `instance.ts`: the once-a-second write.
- `look.ts`: the drawings.
- `play.ts`: the picker, which sends the latest look.
- `screen.ts`: the lobby redraws a changed avatar with a wiggle.
- Unit tests, `simulate.mjs` with dressing traffic, and a browser walk
  including a 360 px phone.

My guess is that it is about the size of v2 item 7.
