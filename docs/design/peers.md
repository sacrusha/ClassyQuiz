# Peers

## Purpose

Friends play a quiz together with nobody running it, so that everyone
plays, including whoever asked the AI for it. In a room
someone steers, a hand presses Play and the Solution, and that hand's
screens list the questions with their solutions; so a Peers room runs on
its own, and whoever opened it plays blind like
the others. What the room does should surprise the players least.

There is no wall at a table or on a call, so each device does the wall's
part.

## Situation

- Friends at a table, on a call or on a sofa, each with a phone, a tablet
  or a laptop. On a call nobody can scan a QR code from another screen.
  Nobody reads the questions aloud, and nobody leads a discussion of the
  spread.
- Plausible assumption: a few friends wait for each other. The advance
  rests on it: everyone ready needs no cadence design, where a clock would
  (backlog Later, "Runs by itself").
- A room that waits for everyone is held by one: a phone left awake on a
  table holds it for good; a player who is slow, or not looking, holds it
  as long. Small children play too: being the consistently slow one is
  frustrating, and for a 1st or 2nd grader auto forward makes it even more
  so.
- Plausible assumption: a group regulates its own waiting when the one
  waited for sees a warning and the one who hurries them is seen doing it
  (Countdown).
- Phones sleep, batteries die, a tab gets closed (Constraints, "Survives
  school Wi-Fi"; `connections.md`): a room that waits for everyone can be
  held by a device that is gone, and one that stops waiting can run through
  its questions while every phone is asleep.
- Someone still has to start the quiz, end it early, fix a wrong solution
  and remove an avatar nobody plays. One person learning those controls is
  easier than everyone learning them, and plausibly whoever opens the room
  has been on the site before.
- Plausible assumption: a ranking after every question carries little
  among friends: with no points for speed the top is mostly ties, the
  player's own score is in the bar already, and the winners jump at the
  solution.
- The devices are personal: each leaves when its player likes, and nobody
  is there to end the room for the others. Only time tells a device that
  left from one that slept. Plausible assumption: friends who want the
  podium again look while still together, within minutes (the room ends
  ten minutes after nobody is at it, `PEERS_EMPTY_MS`).
- The free plan's day is the classes' (backlog, Constraints), so a Peers
  room is held to what a class costs (`hosting-and-limits.md`, "What a
  class costs").

## Not known

- Whether "Waiting for" with avatars helps a room along or reads as
  pressure, and whether one up to 25 s old on a polling device misleads.
- Whether Countdown is found, by children too, and whether whose it is
  reads as a group regulating itself or as one player hurrying another;
  whether 15 s suits a solution as it suits a question.
- Whether the standing in the top bar is seen at all, beside the winners
  jumping.
- Whether the spread showing with the key suits friends; whether points
  for speed are wanted between them.
- Whether ten minutes is the right wait before an empty room ends.
- Seen in headless Edge on 360 px and 390 px phones (up to seven friends)
  and a 1366 px laptop; not on real phones, and not with a real group.
